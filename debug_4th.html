July 10, 2026 Sandhiya_saravanan Panayal Story While I asked the students to describe Panayal and the picture, I noticed they were confused about using is and has. I explained that have/has means to own or possess something. I asked each student to make a sentence using have, such as "I have a pencil." To keep everyone engaged, I turned it into a listening game by asking questions like, "Who has a pen?" after students shared their sentences. I also paused during the activity and asked students to recall what another child had said, encouraging active listening. Once students were comfortable with have, I introduced has using a simple rule: he, she, and it take has. Students then practised sentences such as "Panayal has long hair." and "My father has a bike." The listening game kept the whole class engaged. -- Write 5 sentences using "have" and 5 sentences using "has." Record the sentences and send the audio to the WhatsApp group. Edit
